Research Overview

Research in the Gorman Lab (established 2022) lies at the intersection of evolution, ecology, and physiology, thus we take integrative approaches to understanding factors that shape animal behavior (foraging, breeding, toxicology/disease, and movements), fitness (including associated life history traits and trade-offs), and population dynamics (demographic rates and population structure) to advance basic knowledge and inform management and conservation actions. The overarching goal of the lab is to develop molecule to ecosystem perspectives focusing on the integrative evolutionary ecology of animal systems, some of which are critically important for commercial fisheries and subsistence harvests. Together as a lab group, we incorporate modern, quantitative methods in our work, while honing our communication skills to train the next generation of conservation ecologists. The lab also aims to enhance the public’s interest in nature and understanding of the scientific process. We are always looking for new collaborators and students to join our program in Marine Biology at CFOS/UAF, please reach out to Kristen Gorman if you have interest in our work - a few recent papers are noted below. Thanks for stopping by!

Lab News - Fall 2026

MS student Abby Host successfully defended her MS thesis in Spring 2026, her manuscript titled, Fitness-associated traits of sockeye salmon returning to a glacially dominated watershed is currently in review with the journal Marine and Coastal Fisheries. Big congrats to Abby on her progress and getting her manuscript submitted for review. Abby is continuing on in the lab to work on additional analysis and help with some report writing through 2026.

Alex Borsky is making good progress with his retrospective spatial-temporal analysis of herring and salmon catch data from Prince William Sound, Alaska using a 20-year dataset. Alex won best student poster at the American Fisheries Society, Alaska Chapter meeting in Spring 2026! Alex plans to defend his MS thesis by summer 2027.

Rebecca Lavier has transferred to the PhD program in Marine Biology at UAF. Rebecca is working on an analysis of carry-over effects in tufted puffins using over-winter tracking data from the Gulf of Alaska. Rebecca will be presenting results from her first chapter of her dissertation at the Alaska Bird Conference in December 2026.

Two new MS students joined the lab this fall, Ali McCarron and Ellie Bretscher, see details below on their thesis projects.

Current Lab Group Members

Graduate Students

Ellie Bretscher
Ellie joined the lab in Fall 2026. She holds a BS degree in Environmental Studies from University of Alaska Southeast. Ellie has interests in spatial analysis and seabird science. She has been working for the past two summers with the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge on the Pribilof Islands. This project is in close collaboration with the Aleut Community of St. Paul Island Tribal Government, and was made possible through a funding award by US Fish and Wildlife Service, Tribal Wildlife Grant.


Ali McCarron
Ali joined the lab in Fall 2026. She holds a BS degree in Fish and Wildlife Ecology and Management from Montana State University. Ali grew up in Alaska and has spent the last several years working with Alaska Department of Fish and Game out of Homer on their pink and chum stream escapement surveys. She also assisted US Fish and Wildlife Service (Fairbanks office) with their eider survival studies in western Alaska. Ali will be working on our EVOS Salmon-Herring Interactions project focusing on comparative diet studies to evaluate dietary niche partitioning and overlap between species, as well as prey selectivity comparing diet composition with oceanographic sampling of zooplankton.


Rebecca Lavier
Rebecca joined the lab in Spring 2025 as a MMS student and has now transferred to the PhD program in Marine Biology. Rebecca holds a BS degree in Natural Resources Ecology and Conservation from the University of Idaho, and a MS degree in Wildlife Ecology from Colorado State University. Rebecca has interests in oceanography, seabird ecology, and conservation and is currently developing the full scope of her PhD dissertation research.
